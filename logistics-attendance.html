How logistics attendance works with TheCheckIn

GPS confirms the location. Face recognition confirms the person. No more "I'm here" WhatsApp messages.

1

Set up each depot as a branch

Add each depot or warehouse location in the admin dashboard. Set the GPS geofence radius — typically 200–500 metres. Drivers assigned to a depot must check in from within that zone. Takes 5 minutes to configure each location.

2

Driver checks in at the depot

When a driver arrives at the depot before their run, they open the app and complete a face check-in. The app captures their GPS location at that moment. If they're outside the geofenced zone, the check-in is blocked. Takes 5 seconds.

3

Operations manager sees who's dispatched

The dashboard shows which depots are fully staffed and which have drivers missing. Before the first run starts, you can see if a driver hasn't shown up — and arrange a replacement before deliveries are delayed.

4

Timesheets calculated across all depots

Drivers check out when they return to depot at end of shift. Total hours per driver, per depot, per pay period — all calculated from the timestamps. Export for payroll, or connect to ERPNext for direct sync.

Why logistics teams switch to TheCheckIn

GPS proves the driver was at the depot — not marking from home

With WhatsApp messages or manual registers, a driver can mark themselves present from anywhere. GPS geofencing means the check-in is only accepted when they're physically at the depot. Pair with face recognition and you have both identity and location confirmed.

Multiple depots managed from one dashboard

No need to call each depot manager to ask if their team is in. Open the app and see all depots in one view — who's checked in, who's late, who hasn't arrived. One login covers your entire operation regardless of how many locations you run from.

Drivers who rotate between depots are tracked correctly

Some drivers cover different depot routes on different days. When they check in, their GPS confirms which depot they're at — and that's recorded against their attendance for that day. You always know where each driver started from.

Early starts and varied shift times handled automatically

Logistics shifts often start at 4am, 5am, or split across day and night. Each shift is configured with its own expected start time. Late arrivals are flagged relative to that time — not against a generic 9am default that doesn't fit how logistics actually works.
